KEPT HANDOFF #1

From: Wallet & Cross-Chain Engineer
To: Product Lead
Type: BLOCKER
Priority: HIGH

Summary:
Current Privy documentation confirms Kept's user-owned embedded-wallet, additional-signer, policy and gas-sponsorship architecture, but the accepted weekly automatic-saving guardrail cannot currently be enforced exactly as specified using public Privy stateful policies. Stateful aggregation windows max out at 72 hours, and Privy explicitly warns they are not strict concurrent real-time enforcement. Kept's current Product Outline requires the delegated permission itself—not merely the backend scheduler—to enforce amount/period limits.

Recommendation:
Ask Privy sponsor engineering immediately whether a partner/private policy primitive can enforce a seven-day or weekly transaction/count limit. Continue implementing chain, destination, function, receiver and per-transaction maximum constraints now. If Privy confirms no seven-day primitive exists, Product Lead should choose between adding a minimal onchain automatic-saving period guard or formally modifying the MVP guardrail/claim. I recommend the onchain guard over pretending the backend scheduler is an authorization boundary.

Why:
Using the scheduler alone would directly contradict the accepted architecture. A 72-hour Privy cap also does not equal a seven-day cap. Making the Kept backend a wallet owner to manipulate policies dynamically would introduce a materially worse ownership/custody assumption.

Alternatives considered:

Treat the backend scheduler as the weekly control — rejected; contradicts Product Outline and weakens security.
Dynamically rotate signer/policies from Kept backend — rejected under the current user-owner model and creates undesirable control assumptions.
Use a 72-hour policy and describe it as weekly — rejected; technically false.
Add a minimal onchain period-enforcement guard — viable fallback, but requires Product Lead/Contracts/Security approval.
Obtain an undocumented/partner Privy capability supporting seven days — preferred if sponsor confirms it.

Cross-team impact:

Product/UX: Automation copy cannot claim “maximum once per week is enforced” until resolved.
Contracts: May need a minimal period guard if Privy has no suitable primitive.
Wallet/Intents: Static policy implementation can proceed; stateful weekly rule is blocked.
Backend: Scheduler remains a trigger only, never the permission authority.
Privacy/Security: Must review any fallback guard and authorization-key handling.
Economics/Regulatory: No immediate impact.
QA/Release: Allowed/denied policy tests proceed, but weekly-boundary acceptance test is blocked.

Decision required from Product Lead:
If Privy confirms no seven-day policy primitive, approve either (A) a minimal onchain period guard, recommended, or (B) a deliberate relaxation/change to the accepted MVP automatic-saving enforcement requirement.

Project-context update required:
Kept-Privy-Implementation-Spec-07-Sep-2026.txt, Kept-MVP-Technical-Architecture-07-Sep-2026.txt, and potentially Product-Outline.txt depending on the Product Lead decision.
