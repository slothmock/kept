KEPT HANDOFF #1

From: Privacy, Trust & Security Lead
To: Product Lead
Type: RISK
Priority: HIGH

Summary:
The current technical planning permits commitmentType as possible onchain CommitmentRegistry state. For the fitness and study commitments, the type itself is behavioural information. Publishing it would allow wallet-linked observers to infer that a user participates in fitness or study commitments even though raw evidence and verifier identities remain private.

Recommendation:
Use an opaque/versioned commitment identifier or configuration hash onchain. Keep the human-readable behavioural type offchain unless there is a specific contract requirement and an explicit privacy decision to disclose it.

Why:
The product's privacy thesis is minimum disclosure, and the chain only needs enough information to settle rewards. Public behavioural categorization is not obviously necessary.

Alternatives considered:

Publish cleartext type for simpler indexing.
Publish a coarse generic verification class rather than exact type.
Keep only an opaque ID/hash onchain.

Cross-team impact:

Product/UX: None visible to normal users.
Contracts: CommitmentRegistry storage/event fields may change.
Wallet/Intents: None.
Backend: Must map opaque IDs to private definitions.
Privacy/Security: Reduces wallet-linked behavioural disclosure.
Economics/Regulatory: Reduces unnecessary behavioural-data exposure.
QA/Release: Verify events do not leak type or verifier information.

Decision required from Product Lead:
Yes/no: approve opaque behavioural commitment identity onchain by default.

Project-context update required:
Kept-Product-Outline.txt, Kept-MVP-Technical-Architecture-07-Sep-2026.txt, Kept-MVP-Commitments-Verification-Spec-07-Sep-2026.txt.



KEPT HANDOFF #2

From: Privacy, Trust & Security Lead
To: Product Lead
Type: RISK
Priority: HIGH

Summary:
The MVP architecture allows an authorized Kept signer/oracle to submit reward qualifications. This is acceptable for a hackathon only if the contract, rather than backend convention, limits the damage that signer can cause. Privy delegation has the same principle: the backend may request an action, but policy must independently bound what it can authorize.

Recommendation:
Make two controls explicit MVP acceptance criteria: RewardController must cap the oracle to a pre-funded bounded reward treasury with no authority over saver principal, and Privy delegated policies must constrain the actual vault receiver/spender as well as chain/function/amount/period.

Why:
A compromised backend or authorization key should cause, at worst, bounded unwanted reward allocation or a bounded deposit into the user's own savings position. It must not create a path to redirect user funds or vault shares.

Alternatives considered:

Rely on backend checks only.
Give the oracle/admin broad authority for hackathon convenience.
Enforce the critical limits at the contract/wallet-policy boundaries.

Cross-team impact:

Product/UX: Pause/revoke remains required; no extra crypto UX.
Contracts: Reward caps, duplicate protection and role boundaries become acceptance criteria.
Wallet/Intents: Policy must match the exact deposit/approval ABI and receiver.
Backend: Scheduler/oracle stays request authority, never final permission authority.
Privacy/Security: Limits consequences of infrastructure compromise.
Economics/Regulatory: Keeps behavioural treasury exposure bounded.
QA/Release: Add receiver-redirection, arbitrary-approval, duplicate-reward and over-cap tests.

Decision required from Product Lead:
Yes/no: accept these as mandatory MVP security acceptance criteria.

Project-context update required:
Kept-Privy-Implementation-Spec-07-Sep-2026.txt, Kept-MVP-Technical-Architecture-07-Sep-2026.txt, and the smart-contract acceptance criteria.

Open questions

Decisions made within role authority: The MVP social score, hard-invalidity rules, challenge behavior, privacy-minimal trust inputs, and false-positive policy above.

Assumptions: Social verification remains study sessions; two approvals are required; reward exposure remains small and pre-funded.

Blockers: None for backend implementation. Contract interface freeze should wait on the two Product Lead decisions above.

Dependencies: Backend attestation/schema work, Privy policy implementation, RewardController access-control/cap design, and QA fixtures.

Items requiring Product Lead approval: Opaque onchain behavioural commitment identity; bounded reward-oracle and delegated-signer controls as explicit MVP acceptance criteria.
