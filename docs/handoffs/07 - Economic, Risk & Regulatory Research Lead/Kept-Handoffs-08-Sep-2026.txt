KEPT HANDOFF #1

From: Economics, Risk & Regulatory Research Lead
To: Product Lead
Type: RISK
Priority: HIGH

Summary:
The bounded pre-funded USDC model is suitable for the hackathon, but current UK regulatory research identifies a material production risk: Kept's separate behavioural cash reward may fall within the FCA prohibition on monetary/non-monetary incentives in financial promotions for qualifying cryptoassets. In addition, the current product assumption that regulated providers can own KYC/compliance does not remove Kept's potential independent MLR, future FSMA or CARF obligations where Kept arranges crypto exchange, safeguards assets or otherwise performs an in-scope service.

Recommendation:
Keep the MVP architecture and behavioural treasury unchanged for the hackathon. Adopt 25 USDC/28-day demo treasury, 500 USDC eligible-balance cap, 2% annualised-equivalent maximum formula, 0.75 USDC hard user cap and aggregate reward factor ≤1.0 as provisional economics. Do not approve a UK retail launch or UK-facing reward marketing until specialist UK financial-services counsel maps the exact vault, USDC, Aave, Aurora, Privy and behavioural-reward flows against current and October-2027 regulation.

Why:
The MVP treasury remains solvent and maximum loss is bounded. The regulatory concern is structural rather than numerical: FCA rules can prohibit an incentive regardless of why it is offered, and FCA materials explicitly warn that ongoing rewards may be caught. Current UK MLR rules also cover certain arrangements for cryptoasset exchange, while the broader FSMA crypto regime begins on 25 October 2027.

Alternatives considered:

Remove behavioural rewards from MVP — rejected; destroys the central product proof and is unnecessary for a hackathon prototype.
Pretend rewards are part of base yield — rejected; economically misleading and contrary to accepted Kept principles.
Assume regulated providers absorb all compliance responsibility — rejected; not supported by current UK perimeter rules.
Immediately redesign Kept around a regulated fiat savings provider — premature; production legal analysis should determine whether this is necessary.
Launch UK consumer product unchanged and rely on disclosures — rejected as too risky without counsel.

Cross-team impact:

Product/UX: Avoid guaranteed/fixed APY wording; keep base return and behavioural reward visibly distinct; do not imply FSCS/bank-deposit protection.
Contracts: Preserve hard treasury, user, epoch and authority caps; reward authority must remain unable to reach saver principal.
Wallet/Intents: Document exactly who arranges swaps, controls keys and safeguards assets for perimeter review.
Backend: Maintain time-weighted balance inputs and bounded reward factor; prepare for possible CARF/KYC data requirements without collecting them prematurely.
Privacy/Security: Fitness adapter needs special-category-data review; trust scoring requires DPIA/profiling assessment before production.
Economics/Regulatory: Validate long-term sponsor and regulated-partner models; obtain UK legal advice before go-to-market.
QA/Release: Hackathon copy must not present temporary subsidy as sustainable yield or regulatory safety.

Decision required from Product Lead:
Approve or reject the provisional MVP reward configuration above, and approve UK production launch/marketing as a formal counsel-gated milestone rather than an assumption.

Project-context update required:
If accepted, update Kept-Behavioural-Reward-Economics-Framework with the provisional MVP constants and add the UK financial-promotion/incentive risk plus provider-compliance caveat to the production-gap/regulatory section. Do not rewrite the long-term economics as solved.
